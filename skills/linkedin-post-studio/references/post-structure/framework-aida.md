# AIDA (Attention, Interest, Desire, Action)

Classic direct-response structure. Works for lead generation and offer-focused posts.

## Structure

**Attention.** The opening line stops the scroll with a specific result or number. The reader must feel the pull of what they're about to learn.

**Interest.** The next beat creates friction — surfacing the gap between what the reader expected and what actually happened. The "frustrating part" or counterintuitive detail lives here.

**Desire.** You show the mechanism: the subject line change, the one question, the reframe. Specificity drives saves.

**Action.** A single direct CTA. Often a DM keyword trigger that generates both comment volume and a warm list.

## Full example post

> I sent one email and closed a $40,000 contract in 48 hours.
>
> Here's the frustrating part, I'd been chasing this prospect for 6 months.
>
> The only thing that changed was the subject line.
>
> I stopped writing 'Following up' and started writing 'Thoughts on X' where X was a specific problem they'd mentioned on their podcast.
>
> Reply rate went from 3% to 47%.

## Best for

Lead generation posts anchored to a specific, credible result. Works for service providers, consultants, and SaaS founders showcasing proof.

## Avoid when

The Attention line is generic or unverifiable — AIDA collapses without a specific opening claim.
