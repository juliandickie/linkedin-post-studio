# SLAY (Story, Lesson, Application, You)

Lara Acosta's framework. Works across most content goals because each step earns the next.

## Structure

**Story.** Open with a specific, recent event — a client conversation, a decision, a moment that happened. Concrete details make it credible.

**Lesson.** Extract the single insight the story revealed. One sentence, stated plainly. This is what should stick after someone closes the post.

**Application.** Show how the lesson changes what the reader should actually do. Address their situation, not the person in the story.

**You.** End with a question that invites the reader to respond from their own experience. Opens the post up rather than closing it off.

## Full example post

> Story, Last week a client asked me to cut her LinkedIn team from 4 writers to 1.
>
> Lesson, Headcount isn't the problem when the system is broken.
>
> Application, If your 4 writers are all producing 'nice' content nobody saves, you don't need fewer writers, you need one better system.
>
> You, What does your content process actually look like? Drop your honest answer in the comments.

## Best for

Thought leadership, brand-awareness, and lead-gen posts. The You step consistently drives more comments than a standard CTA.

## Avoid when

You don't have a specific story to anchor it — a vague opening makes the Lesson feel unearned and the Application generic.
