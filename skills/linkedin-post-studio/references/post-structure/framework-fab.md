# FAB (Feature, Advantage, Benefit)

Used for product and service posts. Usually subtle — the framework works best when the selling is implicit rather than declared.

## Structure

**Feature.** State what the product or service actually does. Factual, specific — not a marketing headline. One sentence.

**Advantage.** Explain why that feature is better than the default alternative. This is the "so what" that most product posts skip, jumping straight from feature to benefit without earning the connection.

**Benefit.** State what the reader gets as a result. Frame it in terms of their outcome, not your product's capability.

## Full example post

> Our audit review tool scans 200 pages in under 60 seconds.
>
> Most firms still run these manually, which takes a junior analyst two to three days and introduces human error at the worst possible stage.
>
> That means your team gets to the insight phase on day one, not day three.
>
> If you're running more than 10 audits a month, comment DEMO and I'll show you how it works in practice.

## Best for

Product and service posts where the goal is lead generation without a hard sell. Works well for SaaS, professional services, and tools with a clear efficiency advantage.

## Avoid when

The feature is a commodity — if every competitor offers the same capability, the Advantage step collapses and the post reads as generic promotion.
