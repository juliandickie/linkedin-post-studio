# Negative framing hooks

## Patterns

- `"Stop [action]. Start [better action]."`
- `"Delete [thing] from your [surface]."`
- `"If you're still [doing X], you're losing."`

## Examples

1. "Stop writing 'thoughts?' at the end of your posts."
2. "Delete your LinkedIn headline. Write something humans actually read."
3. "Stop niching down. Start niching 'who.'"
4. "If you're still pitching in the first DM, you're losing 90% of replies."
5. "Stop chasing followers. Start hunting problems."
6. "Delete every word your reader could skip."
7. "Stop using 'us' and 'we.' Start using 'you.'"
8. "If your 'About' section reads like a resume, you're losing every scroll."
9. "Stop asking for referrals. Start making yourself unforgettable."
10. "Delete the 5 most-used words from your pitch. Your close rate will double."

## Mechanism

Loss aversion plus imperative framing. Telling someone to stop doing something they are currently doing creates an immediate gap between their present behavior and an implied better state. The imperative triggers a fast evaluative question -- "Wait, am I doing that?" -- which is the engagement the algorithm rewards.

## When to use

Use when the "stop" is specific and widespread enough that a significant portion of your audience is genuinely doing the thing being called out.

## When to avoid

This format is now slightly overused, so the payoff must be sharp -- if the "start" alternative is obvious or vague, readers feel lectured rather than helped, and the post underperforms.
