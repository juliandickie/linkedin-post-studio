# Listicle hooks

## Patterns

- `"[Number] [things] that [outcome]."`
- `"[Number] [mistakes/lessons] I learned [context]."`
- `"[Number] ways to [outcome] in [timeframe]."`

## Examples

1. "7 lessons from 2,000 consecutive days of posting on LinkedIn."
2. "9 tiny habits that 10x my writing output."
3. "11 cold email mistakes killing your reply rate."
4. "5 frameworks I use to write every post in under 15 minutes."
5. "20 things I wish I knew at 25."
6. "6 LinkedIn features 99% of creators ignore."
7. "8 questions to ask before you quit your job."
8. "13 books that changed how I think about building a business."
9. "4 metrics I check every Monday morning."
10. "10 hours of Claude Code saved me 10 weeks of dev work."
11. "7 red flags in a first sales call."
12. "14 AI tools that replaced a $200K software stack."
13. "12 negotiation tactics I stole from ex-hostage negotiators."

## Mechanism

Numeric specificity triggers cognitive closure: the reader knows exactly what they're committing to before they click. Odd numbers (7, 9, 11) outperform even numbers because they feel more researched and less rounded — a list of 8 or 10 reads as arbitrary; a list of 9 reads as curated. The number also sets a completion expectation, which increases dwell time if the items are genuinely distinct.

## When to use

Use when you have a genuine checklist where each item adds distinct value and the total count is defensible.

## When to avoid

Avoid when one strong story would be more powerful, or when you're padding to hit a number.
