# PAS hooks

## Patterns

- `"[Problem]. [Agitation detail]. [Solution tease]."`

## Examples

1. "You're posting daily. Getting nothing. The hook is the problem."
2. "Your emails are too long. Your CTA is buried. Let's fix both."
3. "Clients are ghosting you. It's not the price. It's the pause before you quote."
4. "Your profile gets views but no DMs. Your headline is the leak."
5. "Cold DMs aren't working. You're pitching before you're present."
6. "You're working 60-hour weeks. Your revenue's flat. The bottleneck is upstream."
7. "Your sales team missed quota again. It's not a skill issue. It's a targeting issue."
8. "You're drowning in meetings. The calendar isn't the problem. The agenda is."

## Mechanism

Self-identification with pain. The reader thinks, "That's me." The three-beat structure -- problem, then the specific reason it persists, then a hint at relief -- keeps each sentence doing a distinct job. The short declarative sentences create a staccato rhythm that feels urgent rather than long-winded, which compels the reader to keep scrolling.

## When to use

Use when you know the audience's pain in specific, plain language -- vague problems produce zero self-identification.

## When to avoid

Avoid when the agitation step exaggerates or manufactures distress that the target reader would not genuinely recognize, as manufactured urgency reads as manipulative and damages trust.
