# Achievement and credential hooks

## Patterns

- `"I [specific achievement]. Here's what I learned."`
- `"After [N years] of [activity], here's what matters."`

## Examples

1. "I've written 2,000+ LinkedIn posts. These 12 rules matter most."
2. "After managing $3.5B in capital, here's the one investing lesson I keep coming back to."
3. "3 years as a Fortune 500 copywriter taught me this."
4. "I closed $1.2M in the last 12 months via LinkedIn DMs."
5. "10,000 cold emails later, the only thing that matters is the first line."
6. "I coached 120 creators past $10K/mo. This is the pattern."
7. "After 400 hours on Zoom sales calls this year, here's what I've noticed."
8. "I've shipped 1,500 A/B tests. Here are the 5 that changed everything."
9. "15 years as a trial lawyer taught me the best way to persuade."
10. "After seeing 10,000 dental crowns, here's what I know about case longevity."

## Mechanism

Authority plus specificity. Receipts hit harder than claims. A precise number -- hours, dollars, posts, clients -- acts as evidence before the argument begins. The reader grants provisional credibility and reads on to find out whether the lesson is as solid as the credential suggests.

## When to use

Use when the achievement is specific, verifiable, and directly tied to the lesson that follows in the body.

## When to avoid

Avoid if it reads as a humblebrag without a lesson -- the credential must serve the reader, not just signal status.
