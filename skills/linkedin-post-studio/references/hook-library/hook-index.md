# Hook library index

## Category briefs (1–2 sentences each)

### Listicle

Use when you have a genuine checklist or ranked set of insights. Odd numbers (7, 9, 11) outperform even ones because they feel more researched and less rounded.

### Story

Use when you have a real incident with a clear before-and-after lesson. Specific time, place, and dialog signal authenticity and drop the scroll defense.

### Statistic

Use when you have a citable data source or proprietary numbers that would surprise the reader. The body must pay off the precision the hook promises.

### Question

Use when you resolve the tension in the body. The Zeigarnik effect keeps the question live in the reader's mind until it's answered, driving "See more" clicks.

### Contrarian

Use when you can defend the take with real evidence. Pattern interrupt plus tribal signaling drives comments from believers and skeptics alike.

### Curiosity gap

Use when the body delivers the specific payoff promised. Loewenstein information gap theory: the missing piece creates tension that earns the "See more" expansion click.

### Prediction

Use when you have domain expertise to back the claim and a defined timeframe. Predictions attract agreement and disagreement — both expand reach.

### PAS

Use when the reader's problem is immediate and visceral. Problem-Agitate-Solution leads with pain, sharpens it, then offers relief — well-suited to audiences that are already problem-aware.

### BAB

Use when the desired outcome is aspirational and the contrast between current state and goal state is large enough to feel motivating.

### Achievement and credential

Use when your result or credential is specific enough to feel earned rather than self-promotional. A concrete number gives context; vague credentials read as bragging without proof.

### Mistake and failure

Use when the failure taught something the reader couldn't learn any other way. The lesson is the delivery, not the emotion.

### Negative framing

Use to stop the scroll by naming what the reader fears. This format is now slightly overused; the body needs genuine insight or it reads as alarmist bait.

## Loading pattern for a typical text post

1. Always load this index file first to identify the right category or categories.
2. Based on the post goal and creator archetype from the table above, select 1–3 categories that match.
3. Load only those sub-files (e.g., `hook-listicle.md`, `hook-story.md`) — do not load all 12.
4. Use the examples in each sub-file as structural inspiration. Do NOT copy them verbatim; copying would fail the Anti-AI constraint and LinkedIn's semantic similarity checks.
