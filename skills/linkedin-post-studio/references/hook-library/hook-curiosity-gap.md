# Curiosity gap hooks

## Patterns

- `"Here's what nobody tells you about [topic]."`
- `"The [number]-word sentence that [outcome]."`
- `"The [specific detail] in [unexpected place] that [outcome]."`

## Examples

1. "The one line in my LinkedIn profile that doubled my inbound DMs."
2. "The 43-second email that closed a $50K deal."
3. "I just learned why most carousels fail on slide 3."
4. "There's one setting in LinkedIn that 99% of users never change."
5. "The 5-word email subject line that gets 67% open rates."
6. "The quietest creator in my niche out-earns all of us."
7. "The cold outreach template I've used for 6 years (still works)."
8. "The single question that filters 80% of bad prospects."
9. "The word I deleted from my pitch that doubled close rate."
10. "What I learned watching a Forbes-30-under-30 go bankrupt."

## Mechanism

Loewenstein information gap theory. The hook names a specific missing piece of information the reader doesn't have but immediately wants. That gap creates tension that can only be resolved by reading further. Specificity is what makes this format work: "one line," "43-second," "5-word" are credible precisely because they're oddly precise.

## When to use

Use when the body delivers the specific payoff the hook promises, with enough detail to justify the buildup.

## When to avoid

Avoid over-promising. This format punishes failed delivery harder than any other — a reader who clicks through and finds the payoff vague will not return.
