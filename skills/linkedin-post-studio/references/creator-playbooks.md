# Creator playbooks and ghostwriter methods

## Justin Welsh — Content Matrix and ContentOS

Welsh's Content Matrix is a 5x8 grid that generates 40 foundational post ideas, expandable to 100+ by varying each cell. The matrix solves the blank-page problem by forcing the creator to systematically cross-reference content type with niche theme before a single word is drafted.

**X-axis (8 content types):** Actionable, Motivational, Analytical, Contrarian, Observation, X vs. Y, Present vs. Future, Listicle.

**Y-axis:** 3–5 niche themes chosen based on the creator's offer and expertise.

Output: the matrix yields 40 base post ideas. Each idea produces 3–4 variations, giving 100+ posts per planning session. Welsh's Creator Funnel: Get Discovered → Build Trust → Deepen Relationship → Monetize. The funnel shape determines which archetypes to post at which growth stage.

Batching and repurposing across channels: one weekly newsletter becomes 6–12 social posts across LinkedIn, X, and Threads. ContentOS is the paid course documenting the system ($150). Welsh's solopreneur business generates approximately $8M/year.

## Jasmin Alic (Hey Jay) — Dear Son method

A voice and rigor technique built around writing to one specific reader.

- **Bracket every post** with "Dear son," at the top and "Love, Dad" at the bottom. Write to that single person. Delete both lines before publishing.
- **Visual suffocation rule:** if the post does not fit on one mobile screen, force the content into a carousel instead.
- **20% cut rule:** delete 20% of the text before posting.

Alic's hooks are typically 5–8 words. He is known specifically for proving — with LinkedIn testing data — that short hooks outperform long ones. Pricing: $1,500 per one-hour coaching session, $5K for 4-week coaching, 500+ member Link Up community.

## Nicolas Cole + Dickie Bush — Ship 30 for 30 and Premium Ghostwriting Academy

**Atomic Essay:** under 250 words, one idea, one visual (image, chart, or diagram), published daily for 30 days. The foundational writing practice taught to tens of thousands of writers since 2020. The constraint forces economy — 250 words is not a ceiling for length, it is a ceiling for scope. One idea per essay is the rule, not a guideline.

**Premium Ghostwriting Academy curriculum:**

- 10-module video course.
- 3 weekly clinics: Niche and Content, Sales, Tech.
- Thursday Hot Seat — live teardown of a student's work.
- Packaging and Pricing module.
- 5 Archetypes of Client Voices: The Professor, The Operator, The Storyteller, The Contrarian, The Insider.

**Voice capture process:** transcribe every client call; revise starting from transcript line-by-line; read the draft aloud in the client's voice before submitting.

**5 Pillars of Digital Writing:** Hooks, Headlines, Hub and Spoke (one long-form piece into many short ones), Habit, Harvesting (resurfacing winning content).

## Lara Acosta — SLAY and Educational Storyteller

- **SLAY:** Story, Lesson, Application, You. The framework sequences personal narrative into niche-specific payoff.
- **Educational Storyteller method:** Platitude (broad hook), Story (personality), Lesson (niche payoff). Designed to draw readers in with a relatable opening, then reward them with specific expertise.
- **Rule of One:** one problem, one solution, one thing per post. Anything that adds a second problem or parallel solution weakens the core.
- **Proof-First content:** 80% of her posts contain a step-by-step framework or a screenshot.
- **Halo Effect content:** post about a bigger creator and tag them. Example: posting lessons learned from Justin Welsh and tagging him.
- Cadence: 3–5 posts per week, not daily. Acosta explicitly criticizes over-posting as a lazy growth strategy.

## Executive archetypes (strategic content model)

Complementary to Cole's 5 Voice Archetypes. Cole's model captures how clients sound; the Executive Archetypes model captures how clients think about their business. This distinction matters for content strategy because the three archetypes require different pillar mixes, cadence, and proof styles.

**Visionary:**

- Focus: long-term strategy, 30,000-foot views, creative energy.
- Natural content: industry shifts, predictions, "mantra" beliefs, category creation.
- Strong pillars: Perspective, Personal (origin and conviction stories), Participation (debate-inviting questions).
- Weaker pillars: Processes and Proof — tactical operator content often feels off-brand.
- Example executives: VCs, category-defining founders, innovation consultants.
- Sample mix: 40% Perspective, 25% Personal, 15% Participation, 10% Proof, 10% Problems.

**Operator:**

- Focus: execution, task completion, efficiency metrics.
- Natural content: workflow breakdowns, day-in-the-life problem-solving, specific tooling choices, system diagrams.
- Strong pillars: Processes, Problems, Proof.
- Weaker pillars: big-vision posts often feel forced unless anchored in specific execution lessons.
- Example executives: COOs, heads of operations, agency owners running delivery, clinical practice managers.
- Sample mix: 30% Processes, 25% Problems, 20% Proof, 10% Personal, 10% Perspective, 5% Participation.

**Processor (Analyst):**

- Focus: data, detail, risk assessment.
- Natural content: research findings, myth-busting with data, benchmark commentary, decision frameworks with explicit trade-offs.
- Strong pillars: Proof, Perspective (when data-backed), Processes (as decision models).
- Weaker pillars: first-person vulnerability stories can feel forced and out of character.
- Example executives: CFOs, actuaries, quants, data-forward consultants, evidence-based clinicians.
- Sample mix: 30% Proof, 25% Perspective, 20% Processes, 15% Problems, 5% Personal, 5% Participation.

Implementation note: assess archetype during onboarding before building the content calendar. Many content failures come from forcing a Visionary-style calendar on an Operator client, or vice versa. The archetype should drive pillar weighting, hook patterns, and which formats the client pushes vs. avoids.

## Voice capture interview prompts

The quality of raw interview material determines the ceiling on every post that follows. Generic prompts produce generic content.

**Decision and Rationale** (highest-leverage prompt): instead of "What do you think about X?", ask "Tell me about a difficult decision you made about X and why you picked that path." The second version forces specifics, stakes, and rationale. The first almost always produces a platitude.

**Additional high-yield prompts:**

- "What belief do you hold about your industry that most peers would disagree with?"
- "Tell me about a decision you made last month and the rationale behind it."
- "What do people oversimplify in your field that annoys you?"
- "What was the single biggest metric that moved the needle this week?"
- "What's a question a client asked you recently that you didn't have a clean answer to?"
- "What did you think was true 5 years ago that you now think is wrong?"
- "What's the worst advice in your industry that people still follow?"

Run these monthly in 15–30 minute recorded sessions. Each session transcript fuels 4–8 posts. Record every session without exception — memory degrades quickly, and the specific language the client uses in the moment is the most valuable raw material. Paraphrasing that language in the draft is one of the most common voice-capture failures.

## The Three Passes drafting method

Ghostwriters who scale to 5+ clients use a three-pass protocol that separates research, structure, and polish into discrete phases.

**Pass 1 — Transcript and notes:** Extract raw insights from the voice interview. Direct quotes, metaphors, specific examples, numbers the client mentioned. No editing or structuring at this stage. Output: bullet list of usable raw material.

**Pass 2 — Outline and framework selection:** Assign each raw insight to a framework (PAS, BAB, listicle, story-to-lesson, contrarian-authority). Draft the hook line and the closing line. Leave the body as structured bullets. Validate that the post has one core idea, one intended reader action, and hook-body congruence. Output: skeleton with hook, body bullets, and CTA specified.

**Pass 3 — Final draft:** Expand body bullets into prose using the client's voice file. Apply the 210-character hook rule and mobile formatting. Apply the Anti-AI constraint (see `anti-ai-constraint.md`). Cut 20% before submitting. Output: post ready for client review.

This structure prevents two common failures: drafting prose before structure is locked (which produces well-written posts with no payoff), and skipping voice application (which produces structurally sound posts that sound nothing like the client).

## Batching practices

Welsh: one newsletter per week chopped into 6–12 social pieces. Alic: 2–3 hours per week in a single block. Acosta: weekly batch, 3–5 posts.

**Standard sprint structure:**

- 3-hour block for 30 ideas.
- 4-hour block for 10–15 drafts.
- 2-hour block to polish and schedule.

Batching in single sessions protects voice consistency — switching context between clients or between writing and other work introduces inconsistency that revision cannot fully correct. A 9-hour writing day spread across 5 clients is less effective than a 3-hour block dedicated to one client.
