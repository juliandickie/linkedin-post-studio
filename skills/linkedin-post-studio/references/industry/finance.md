# Finance — Content patterns and compliance

## What works

Finance on LinkedIn operates in two tiers with meaningfully different compliance constraints: registered representatives and investment advisers under close firm supervision, and non-registered commentators — fintech operators, financial journalists, educators — with considerably wider latitude.

**For registered professionals: data commentary without personalized advice.** The highest-performing content from registered advisers and RIAs analyzes market conditions or investment category behavior at the population level. "Market P/E ratios haven't been this stretched since 2000. Here is what that meant for forward 10-year returns the last two times" is educational content grounded in historical data, not a recommendation directed at a specific reader's portfolio.

**For non-registered commentators: analytical frameworks and contrarian takes.** Fintech founders, financial educators, and financial journalists thrive on framework-based content. Ben Carlson's and Morgan Housel's posts demonstrate how to generate substantial reach with no compliance overlay, provided claims are not directed at readers as investment recommendations.

**Economic and market explainers.** Translating employment reports, CPI releases, and Fed decision language into accessible analysis generates high engagement. The anchor is the data itself, not an implied trade.

**Financial planning frameworks.** Content explaining how a category of investor thinks through tax-loss harvesting, Roth conversion windows, or sequence-of-returns risk attracts planners and advisers. The framing: "here is the framework," not "here is what you should do."

**Industry and sector analysis.** Commentary on interchange fee economics, BNPL risk models, or fixed income market structure attracts in-house practitioners and fintech builders underserved by mainstream financial media.

## What fails

**Personalized investment recommendations.** "Based on current rates, you should move into X" is the clearest violation pattern. Forward-looking statements that imply action create regulatory exposure for registered professionals and reputational risk for everyone.

**Performance claims without required disclosures.** "My model portfolio is up 34% this year" without period specification, benchmark comparison, and fee disclosure violates both the SEC Marketing Rule and FINRA content standards. March 2025 SEC FAQ updates tightened net versus gross performance presentation requirements.

**Cherry-picked testimonials.** Using only favorable testimonials without the required disclosure framework violates the Marketing Rule's cherry-picking prohibition. Testimonials require disclosure of client status, compensation, and — for promoters receiving more than $1,000 annually — a written agreement.

**Liking or sharing third-party content as a registered representative.** Under FINRA Notice 17-18, liking or sharing constitutes adoption and entanglement. The firm becomes responsible for the shared content. Most compliance programs prohibit this without review.

**Static content without pre-approval.** LinkedIn profiles, pinned posts, and featured sections are retail communications under FINRA Rule 2210 requiring principal pre-approval. Many broker-dealer employees update their profiles without recognizing this obligation.

## Industry-specific hook adaptations

Finance hooks perform best when they open with a data point or a behavioral observation that does not imply a specific action. The hook signals analytical authority without crossing into advice.

**Example 1 — The historical data pattern:**
"Market P/E ratios haven't been this stretched since 2000.
Here is what forward 10-year returns looked like both times."

**Example 2 — The behavioral observation hook:**
"I've reviewed 300 financial plans this year.
The single most common mistake has nothing to do with investment selection."

**Example 3 — The structural change opener:**
"The Fed's rate path just changed the math on fixed-rate mortgages.
Here is what it means for buyers making decisions in the next 90 days."

**Example 4 — The contrarian take:**
"Index fund maximalism is a generational default, not a strategy.
Here is what that distinction actually costs people over 30 years."

## Industry-specific CTA patterns

Finance CTAs for regulated professionals must direct engagement away from personalized advice and toward education, peer commentary, or qualified consultation.

**Save-bait for framework content:**
"Save this framework for your next client planning conversation. Educational content, not advice for any specific situation."

**Peer engagement close for professionals:**
"What is the planning implication you're hearing most often from clients on this? Tracking responses for a piece I'm writing."

**Disclaimer-forward soft close:**
"This is educational content, not investment advice. Past performance does not guarantee future results. My opinions are my own, not those of [firm]. Consult a qualified adviser for your situation."

**Resource CTA for non-regulated commentators:**
"Full data sources in the first comment. Everything is FRED and BLS, open access."

## Compliance rules

**SEC Marketing Rule 206(4)-1.** Adopted December 2020 under the Investment Advisers Act of 1940, with a compliance deadline of November 4, 2022. The rule's seven general prohibitions cover: untrue statements of material fact; unsubstantiated material claims; misleading implications; unfair treatment of risks versus benefits; cherry-picking of performance periods; misleading performance data; and otherwise misleading content. SEC updated its FAQs on March 19, 2025, clarifying net versus gross performance presentation and the treatment of hypothetical performance.

**Testimonials under the Marketing Rule.** Testimonials are permitted if they include clear disclosure of whether the person is a current or former client and whether compensation was provided. A written agreement is required when a promoter receives more than $1,000 in a rolling 12-month period. A disqualification check is required before engaging any promoter.

**FINRA Rule 2210.** Governs all communications with the public by broker-dealers. Static LinkedIn content — profiles, pinned posts, featured sections — constitutes retail communications requiring principal pre-approval. Interactive posts (status updates, comments) are excluded from pre-approval under the interactive exception but remain subject to content standards and Rule 3110 supervision obligations.

**FINRA Regulatory Notices.** Notice 10-06 covers blogs and social media. Notice 11-39 covers hyperlinks and the adoption/entanglement doctrine. Notice 17-18 establishes that liking or sharing third-party content triggers adoption, making the firm responsible for that content. Notice 19-31 provides updated guidance on digital communications.

**Recordkeeping.** SEA 17a-3/17a-4 and FINRA Rule 4511 require broker-dealers to retain business communications for at least 3 years. Investment advisers under Rule 204-2 must retain for 5 years. LinkedIn posts that constitute business communications must be archived in a WORM-compliant system. Certified compliance partners include Smarsh, Global Relay, Hearsay, and Proofpoint.
