# Formatting and visual best practices

## Unicode characters used by top creators

Specific characters that recur across top-performing posts, with their best-use context:

- **→** (right arrow) — pivots and transitions. "Step 1 → Step 2."
- **↳** (corner arrow) — sub-point under a heading; creates visual indentation.
- **•** (bullet) — neutral list marker.
- **▸** (filled triangle) — emphasis bullet.
- **✅** (check) — positive or "do" items.
- **❌** (X) — negative or "don't" items.
- **♻️** (recycle) — "repost this" CTA.
- **📌** (pin) — "save this."
- **💡** (lightbulb) — insight marker.
- **🎯** (target) — goal.
- **👇** (down arrow) — points to link below.
- **P.S.** — second-read hook. Most readers scan to the bottom after expanding the post. A strong P.S. line is a high-performing placement for a secondary CTA or a final twist. Treat it as a discrete content slot, not an afterthought.

## When to use emojis

- **Sparingly:** 1–8 per post.
- **As visual bullets,** not decoration.
- **Never in the hook.** An emoji as the first character of a post reads amateur in 2025–2026.
- **Flag emojis and skin-tone variants** count as 4–7 characters each toward the 3,000-character limit — they consume budget faster than standard Unicode.

**Industry-specific emoji norms:**

- **B2B SaaS:** minimal. Treated as unprofessional in enterprise-adjacent contexts.
- **Creator economy:** moderate. Emojis reinforce casual tone.
- **Healthcare:** very minimal. Medical content needs to read serious.
- **Legal:** near-zero emoji use.
- **Finance:** minimal. Compliance risk when emoji creates ambiguity in regulated claims.
- **Fitness and lifestyle:** more permissive.

## Image aspect ratios

- **Square 1200×1200 (1:1):** safest default. Works across feed, profile card, and share previews.
- **Portrait 1200×1500 (4:5):** maximum mobile screen real estate, highest engagement, and the default for text overlay posts. On a phone, this fills the feed better than any other ratio.
- **Landscape 1200×627 (1.91:1):** used for link previews and horizontal quote cards.
- **Minimum** 1080×1080. **Maximum** 4320×4320.
- **Format:** JPEG or PNG, under 5 MB.

Portrait/vertical outperforms landscape by 10–40% (§1 data) because 72% of LinkedIn engagement happens on mobile. When in doubt, go portrait.

## Text overlay practices

- **Bold high-contrast type** — readable on mobile without zooming.
- **One main point per image,** two maximum. More than two points and none of them land.
- **2–3 brand colors, 2 fonts maximum.** Visual consistency across a series creates a recognizable format.
- **10–15% padding** for crop safe zone — important for mobile rendering where edges can be clipped.
- **Author attribution in bottom corner.** Establishes ownership when the image is shared outside LinkedIn.
- **Keep source visible on screenshot proof.** When using screenshots as social proof (engagement counts, email subject lines, DM previews), the source should be legible. Sahil Bloom's signature tactic: use Tweetpik for clean tweet screenshots rather than raw phone captures.

## Line break rules

- **Blank line between every paragraph.** Dense blocks of text without breaks are among the fastest ways to lose mobile readers.
- **Single line break inside a list.** Items stay visually grouped.
- **Two line breaks between major sections.** Creates breathing room between distinct ideas.
- **Spaces count toward the 3,000-character limit.** Blank lines are not free — account for them in your character budget.

## Character budget notes

The character budget interacts directly with the above-the-fold rules from `algorithm-mechanics.md`:

- **Mobile visibility cutoff:** ~140 characters (2 lines). Everything above this line is the hook — the only text a mobile user sees before deciding whether to tap "See more."
- **Desktop visibility cutoff:** ~210 characters (3 lines).
- These cutoffs mean the hook must do its work within the first 140 characters on mobile. Formatting choices inside the hook — emoji, symbols, line breaks — all consume that budget. A two-line hook that burns 80 characters on a symbol or emoji leaves little room for the actual promise.
- The highest-engagement zone for the whole post is 1,200–2,000 characters (AuthoredUp/Socialinsider data). Formatting decisions — blank lines, bullet characters, P.S. placement — all affect where the post lands in this range. Aim to use visual structure to fill this range deliberately, not accidentally.
